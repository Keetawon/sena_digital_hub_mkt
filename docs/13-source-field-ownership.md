## Ownership and identity

REM owns SENA's approved source identifiers, internal names, and operational relationships. Hub receives them through the existing DW and preserves them read-only. DW is the transport and evidence layer, not a new owner of those facts. The governed commercial Sheet supplies commercial enrichment through `gold.dim_project`; those values retain their distinct origin. Hub owns separately approved public wording, content, assets, and publication history.

Every entity keeps its Hub UUID and stable public identifier. External identifiers remain separate attributes and integration keys; they do not replace Hub primary keys or public API path keys. Preserve source IDs and codes as strings, including leading zeros. Preserve source names exactly; any normalized matching value is separate from the original. Names are never identity keys.

Canonical residential brands, projects and source models retain required REM references. The approved commercial draft catalogue adds separately identified Marketing types within an existing project, including types awaiting source association; it does not select another master system or create a publishable non-REM project. Its initial storage/media increment remains private draft work. Reviewed lineage, content publication and verified unit partition are separate decisions; an approved REM identity alone approves none of them.

Company remains read-only source context, shown in collapsed reference details when available. It is not the Marketing hierarchy, a new required input or a completeness task. Management/team changes must not recreate project/type/asset IDs. This simplification does not edit source CompanyID, waive source-integrity checks, or change legal disclosure and company-targeted promotion requirements.

The DW's `dim_unit.unit_type_commercial` is an alias of the project model's `ModelName`, joined on both project and model IDs. It is not evidence of a separate, standardized studio / one-bedroom / two-bedroom taxonomy. The inspected model contract selects `ModelID`, not `ModelCode`; this does not prove that no separate model-code field exists elsewhere in REM.

Use explicit terms in the intake contract: **REM project model code** (`ModelID` scoped by `ProjectID`), **Marketing Unit Type** (separate Hub identity), **model-type reference** (`ModelTypeID`), **REM UnitTypeID**, and **individual unit** (`UnitID`). The separate UnitTypeID field exists on `Sys_REM_ProjectModelOtherData`, but the historical KUBON/B_PO checks supplied no values. Its primary key is `ID`, not `(ProjectID, ModelID)`, so do not assume a single classification row per model. The separate `Sys_Master_UnitType` table uses `(SBUID, UnitID, UnitType)` and is not an interchangeable UnitTypeID dictionary. See the [historical UnitTypeID resolution](14-pilot-intake-kubon-b-po.md#business-terminology-resolution) for the evidence; the 20 September decision refines the commercial terminology. That empty UnitTypeID path is not a blocker for source-model identity. Specifications and operational membership still need separately verified evidence.

For staff wording, “แบบขาย” identifies the Marketing type and “รหัสแบบ (REM)” identifies an exact immutable source ModelID. Label the older canonical-model editor explicitly when it is shown alongside the new commercial draft catalogue. In the historical KUBON/B_PO snapshot all 96 source ModelNames happen to equal their ModelIDs; that equality is not a general identity rule. Preserve variants such as `A1-1L` and `A1-1R` as separate keys even when one commercial type references both, and never merge a code across projects.

`gold.dim_project_model` groups active technical rows by exact trimmed `(project_id, model_name)` and retains their `model_ids` as a JSON array. Its grouped name/key is useful for presentation, not a replacement for the canonical `(ProjectID, ModelID)` mapping. Area ranges and `model_attribute_variant` describe source differences, not approved bedroom counts or layout equivalence.

Project master plans belong to projects/phases; building/floor plans belong to spatial applicability. The spatial-tree requirements remain; detailed variant controls are secondary. Draft attachments still require rights, applicability and publication review before release.

## Residential intake and change handling

1. Use the approved pilot ProjectID list. Collect each project's exact brand, available company evidence and `(ProjectID, ModelID)` rows. Company validation is source work, not an extra Marketing entry task. Broad REM project-type codes or a “not deleted” flag alone do not establish residential scope.
2. Validate keys, references, source status, row counts, extraction time, and the versioned DW dataset contract before making an import available for selection. Do not turn a missing source reference into a synthetic REM identity.
3. Preserve the source copy, then initialize separate public names and enrich Hub-owned content. Human confirmation of model mappings remains mandatory under REM-06; exact IDs do not bypass it.
4. If a field is absent, record the gap and its owner. A known source-owned field must be corrected by that source owner. New Hub-owned information may be supplied with evidence and the applicable approval; it must not impersonate an imported value.
5. Refresh source evidence and raise drift on a successful changed snapshot. Do not overwrite public fields or immutable releases. Follow REM-09..11 for changed/missing records and sync failures, plus the specific brand and promotion eligibility rules in CONT-06 and PROMO-43; those exceptions must not be replaced by a blanket unpublish rule.
6. Publish only after identity, mapping, content and rights checks pass. Website/API consumers and AI receive the approved release, not the raw import.

## Future businesses without REM — Phase 2 policy

These options describe later onboarding decisions, not Phase 1 features or permission to load Solar/EV/LivNex now.

| Future business situation | Authoritative catalogue | Onboarding policy |
|---|---|---|
| Another ERP or approved master catalogue already exists | That system and its named business owner | Preserve its existing identifiers and approved metadata; map them to stable Hub identities |
| An approved catalogue exists only in a spreadsheet | The named owner controls the catalogue and its code/version rules | Validate and review imports; the file format alone does not establish authority |
| No master catalogue exists | Hub may own the catalogue after the business owner explicitly assigns that responsibility | Authorised users create records, a designated owner approves codes/names/specifications, and publication follows the approved workflow |

Do not create fake REM IDs, empty-code placeholders, or residential projects just to host another business's products or services. A SENA residential record awaiting REM registration remains subject to REM-08 and cannot use the future non-REM path to publish early.

When Phase 2 is scoped, external references must distinguish source system, source instance, entity type and complete source key, including any project scope. This prevents identical codes from different systems from colliding. A new source can later be linked to an existing Hub entity without recreating its public identity, assets or history. A source migration needs reviewed identity matching, an explicit field-ownership transition and conflict handling; attaching a reference must not silently transfer ownership or replace public content.

Future products/services need models based on their actual business meaning. Shared assets, provenance and approval principles can carry forward, but we will not build a generic product schema, adapter framework or BU workspace before those requirements are confirmed. Hub ownership of a catalogue does not automatically make Hub the authority for operational stock, pricing, contracts or customer records.
